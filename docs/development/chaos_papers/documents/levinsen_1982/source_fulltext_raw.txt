Impact on parametric amplifiers? 
M. T. Levinsen 
Physics Laboratory I, H. C. Orsted Institute, Uniuersitetsparken 5, DK-2100 Copenhagen 0, Denmark 
(Received 8 September 1981; accepted for publication 9 December 1981) 
The Stewart-McCumber model of a Josephson junction has been shown to exhibit period-
doubling bifurcation cascades, as described by the Feigenbaum bifurcation theory. Chaotic 
states, sometimes associated with the bifurcations, are also prevalent. The present paper deals 
with the questions of sub harmonic generation and chaotic states in the aforementioned model, 
and in addition with the problem of the ubiquitous noise rise found in Josephson junction 
parametric amplifiers. The bifurcation is first discussed by drawing on analytical results on the 
Duffing equation which is an approximation to the complete ac-driven Stewart-McCumber 
model. The complete model is then solved on an analog computer. Thereafter it is shown that 
besides the even subharmonics predicted by the bifurcation theory, the natural subharmonic to 
expect at small de currents is the odd. This may then have associated its own bifurcation tree. 
The role of spon taneous symmetry breaking will be discussed. This reconciles the earlier 
treatment of the 3-photon amplifier with the Feigenbaum scheme. Finally, analog calculations 
on a model ofan externally pumped Josephson junction parametric amplifier will be discussed. 
The conclusion seems to be that chaotic noise cannot account for the noise rise.

I. INTRODUCTION 
The existence of period-doubling bifurcation cascades 
in the Stewart-McCumber I model of a Josephson junction 
has been pointed out by Huberman et oJ. 2 Since then a rather 
extensive study of the conditions for the cascading bifurca-
tions in this model (see Fig. 1) has been performed by Kautz. 3 
It is the aim here first to provide a more detailed understand-
ing of the connection between the earlier theories4 for para-
metric amplification on the supercurrent (and the micro-
wave induced constant voltage steps) and the cascading 
bifurcation theory of Feigenbaum.s Especially the role of 
spontaneous symmetry breaking will be treated in detail. In 
Ref. 4 it was shown that the ac-driven Stewart-McCumber 
model could be unstable towards generation of a half-har-
monic. For this to happen conditions were ide :;60 and the 
pump frequency fD approximately equal to twice the J oseph-
son plasma frequency /p. The condition ide :;60 came about 
by proving that the threshold pump power for ide = 0 was 
infinite. In Ref. 6 a symmetry argument was similarly em-
ployed to rule out the possibility of 3-photon interaction for 
ide = O. Since in Ref. 2 it was found that bifurcation took 
place for ide = 0 and a condition was/D </p, obviously some 
clarification is needed for surely the two phenomena are one 
and the same. Secondly, it will be demonstrated that the 
natural subharmonic to expect is really the odd sub harmonic 
and not the even that comes about as an instability. Associat-

In Ref. 2 a very complicated pattern of deterministic 
chaos, sometimes associated with the bifurcation cascades, 
was also observed. This chaotic noise was pointed to in Ref. 2 
as a likely source of the noise rise always encountered in 
externally pumped Josephson junction parametric amplifi-

of chaotic states. In order to test the idea a complete amplifi-
er was modeled on an analog computer. Both the 3- and 4-
photon amplifier cases were treated. Also the existence of the 
diverse sub harmonics was investigated with the help of the 
analog computer. As in the cases of Refs. 2 and 3 the basic 
junction model will be the ac-driven Stewart-McCumber 
model. This is rather difficult to treat analytically and we 
shall therefore take recourse to the Duffing equation to give 
a qualitative understanding of what to expect and then use 
an analog computer (EMRI-ACS-122 D hybrid analog com-
puter) to verify the conclusions on the complete model.

V. AMPLIFIER NOISE 
In all externally pumped parametric amplifiers based 
on the Josephson junction one encounters the strange phe-
nomenon of the noise rise which at least for tunnel junctions 
seems to have the unfortunate consequence that gain and 
noise temperature are proportional. For a detailed review of 
this we should refer to Ref. 10. For the microbridge at least 
the range of parameters is such that it seems very unlikely 
that chaotic noise might occur. The RC time constant corre-
sponds to a frequency of order 500 GHz. The pump frequen-
cy is therefore well below the lower cut-off frequency for 
chaotic behavior. The phase-instability noise of Ref. 11 
seems therefore a good candidate for explaining the noise 
rise here although some difficult points remain. 10 We should 
remark here that although the exact coefficient is not yet 
known, a gain-dependent noise temperature is also found in 
the case of the microbridge. In what follows we shall devote 
ourselves to the question of tunnel junctions where chaotic 
noise may be expected to occur. 
Since for the 4-photon amplifier one is actually able to 
choose junction parameters such that to operate outside the 
regime of chaotic behavior, we shall concentrate on the 3-
photon amplifier in the following. There is of course no es-
caping that the operating point has to be close to the very 
threshold of 1/2 harmonic excitation and therefore may be 
comparatively close to a regime of chaotic behavior, con-
nected with a cascading bifurcation. In the calculation de-

From this we conclude that the chaotic noise, although 
itself an intriguing phenomenon, does not seem to explain 
the observed noise rise neither for the 3- nor for the 4-photon 
amplifier. We have altogether not been able to produce the 
noise rise on the analog. One reason may be the comparative-
ly low Q ( - 10) of the tuned circuit as compared to that in real 
experiments, where amplifier bandwidths of order 20 MHz 
are encountered compared to signal frequencies of 35 GHz. 
Such high Q values, however, are very difficult to realize on 
the analog. Also their occurrence in experiments is not fully 
accounted for. It is anyway difficult to see that a high Q 
could bring about the coexistence of gain and chaotic noise, 
but this could be the explanation for the failure of the mecha-
nism suggested in Ref. 14 to show up.
